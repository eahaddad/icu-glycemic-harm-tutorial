## The leakage assertion: 948 rows, and the rule that fixed it

The assertion in 02 counts rows whose `max_feature_time` is at or after t_k. The first run returned 948. Every medication, feed, ventilation, dialysis, culture and vital component takes its time from a charttime or a segment start that the join already requires to be before t_k, so the only components that could have produced a later time were the two whose join used a different column: the glucose stream and the lab panel enter on `storetime < t_k` (a result is visible once the lab system stores it) while the time carried into `max_feature_time` is the charttime (the nominal time of the measurement). A value stored at 09:58 and charted as 10:05 around a landmark at 10:00 passed the join and set `max_feature_time` after t_k. The information was available before the landmark; the row still claims to see nothing timed after it, and a value timed after the landmark has no place in it.

The rule is now that a glucose or lab value enters only when both its storetime and its charttime are before t_k. Table 1b in 02 measures the phenomenon the fix removes: per source, how many results carry a charttime later than their storetime, by how many minutes at the median and the 90th percentile, and how many landmark rows had such a value straddling t_k (stored before it, timed at or after it). Its row count across the two sources should add up to the 948 of the first run, allowing for rows that straddle in both sources; the re-run reports it next to a leakage count of 0. The planted case in `tests/make_synthetic.py` is a fingerstick entered at 09:58 and charted at 10:05 around patient 1's first landmark at 10:00: under the old rule the harness reports 1 violating row, under the new one 0 and Table 1b reports it as the one straddling value.

## Table 12: where an insulin drip is visible, per era

The hypothesis the first run put in doubt is that the inputevents drip segments are the record of insulin infusions in every era. Table 10 shows inputevents insulin on half of the eligible stays through 2019 and on 19% in 2020 to 2022, while the eMAR insulin bolus share rose to 51%, and Table 2 shows the drip share of landmark rows falling from 21% to 8% in the same era. Two readings fit: the test era charts fewer insulin infusions (less tight control, a different unit mix in the data added by v3.0), or it charts them somewhere else, on the eMAR as Started / Restarted / Stopped events (00 sections D and F found 10,120 Started and about 8,600 Stopped rows), which the at-risk rule does not read. Table 12 counts, per era over the same eligible stays as Table 10, the stays with an inputevents drip row, with an eMAR insulin infusion start inside the stay, with both and with each alone, and the stays with a Confirmed insulin row. If `pct_emar_only` rises in 2020 to 2022 to where `pct_inputevents_drip` fell, the drips moved and the at-risk rule must read eMAR infusion events as exposure too (an interval from Started to the next Stopped or Started, capped, the 00 F construction); if it stays near zero, the test era has fewer drips and the model is tested on a population with fewer infusions, which the evaluation then reports by exposure group.

## Table 13: what an eMAR "Confirmed" insulin row is

00 section D2 read "Confirmed" (58,450 rows, 58,428 of them on the generic medication "Insulin", 0.5% with a dose, 100% with a product row) as a nurse confirming self-administered insulin, the pump patients, and 01 carries it as `self_admin`, which 02 turns into `n_insulin_self_admin_24h`. The 02 profile put that flag on 38.5% of landmark rows with a drip running against 6.9% without one, and Table 5 flagged 13.3% of eMAR insulin doses. Pump patients are a few percent of an ICU, and a running drip is where a pump is disconnected, so the reading does not fit the numbers. The alternative is that Confirmed is the eMAR's record of an infusion check, the nurse confirming the bag or the rate at the hourly glucose. Table 13 tests it directly: for every Confirmed insulin row on a cohort admission, whether its charttime falls inside an inputevents insulin drip segment with an hour of tolerance either side, whether the admission has any drip, and whether the parent row's `administration_type` names an infusion, grouped by that type so the vocabulary is read rather than assumed. If most rows sit inside a drip under an infusion type, `self_admin` is renamed to what it is (an infusion confirmation, redundant with `drip_running_at_tk` and dropped from the feature set, or kept as a charting-intensity signal under its real name) and the Confirmed rows leave `sc_doses`, where `bolus_given` currently counts them as a dose without units; the pump rows, identifiable by the medication text ("for Insulin PUMP", "subcutaneous insulin pump"), keep the self-administration flag. If the rows sit outside any drip, the D2 reading stands and the flag keeps its name.

## Why the eMAR intervals are built the way they are

The eMAR records a drip as events (00 D, F): Started or Restarted when the bag is hung, Confirmed at the nurse's checks, Stopped when it ends. An interval opens at a start event, or at a Confirmed when no interval is open (a drip whose start was charted before the admission's rows or elsewhere), and closes at the first Stopped after it, at the next start, or `INFUSION_CAP_HOURS` after its last start or confirmation when nothing closes it. The cap started at 48 h, the one assumption without a measured basis beyond 00 F (84% of starts closed by a Stopped, median 15.5 h, p90 41.7 h, the unclosed 16% running to discharge), and the third run set it to 16 h from the gaps inside closed intervals (below); table 15 prints, per era, how the intervals end, their lengths, the confirmations inside them, the longest gap between consecutive events inside closed intervals (the evidence for a shorter cap), the share opened by a Confirmed with no Started, and the share overlapping an inputevents segment. The synthetic data plants both cases: patient 2's eMAR Started and Stopped beside the inputevents segments (one drip, seen twice, counted once in every flag) and patient 6's Started and Confirmed with no Stopped (closed by the cap, running at the landmark, the test era's pattern).

Where both sources describe the same drip, 02 takes the larger of the two hours figures and the inputevents rate; an eMAR-only drip has `drip_hours_24h` > 0 and NULL rate and unit features, which the notebook's missing indicators carry. This is a stated limit of the test era: rate features exist for fewer of its drips, and the evaluation reports the drip rows by source.

## Fifth run: a bug of mine, and two tables that still count

The fifth run built 118,257 rows, 10,034 fewer than the fourth, and the drop was not the data. The flowsheet-first condition on the at-risk join tested the exposure's source, and the eMAR doses carry the same source label as the eMAR drip intervals, so in every stay with a flowsheet drip the subcutaneous eMAR doses stopped counting as exposure. The rows lost were the later days of those stays, when patients have come off the drip and are on subcutaneous insulin (the per-day table lost 1% of day-2 rows and 8 to 10% of the rows from day 3 on). The synthetic data did not catch it because its one flowsheet stay has an inputevents-only bolus on the same day. The condition now reads `NOT is_drip OR source != 'emar' OR NOT stay_has_inputevents_drip`, and patient 1 carries a six-hour flowsheet drip on the first evening so that its three later landmarks are at risk only through eMAR doses in a flowsheet stay; under the old condition the harness prints 1 test row instead of 4. None of this run's cohort tables (1 to 3, 5 to 9, 11, 15) are used; two tables are, because the rows they describe are the ones the bug did not touch or because their denominators are stays, not rows.

## What is still open

The cohort rules are closed with the sixth run; what follows is carried into the modeling notebook.

- TRAIN_ERAS: Table 2 says the rates are flat from 2011 on and the first era runs higher; whether 2008 to 2010 trains with the rest is decided in the notebook from the per-era calibration, and 2017 to 2019 is reported as a second held-out era.
- The 930-unit bolus day: the per-dose cap of 200 units passes a day of several large doses. The notebook winsorises the daily sums; the SQL keeps the raw sum so the choice can be audited.
- The 61 fingerstick zeros that would have been < 40 events: a stated limit, with the sensitivity run.
- `INFUSION_CAP_HOURS` is 16 h on the strength of the closed-interval gaps; with flowsheet first it governs only the stays without a flowsheet record, mostly 2020 to 2022.
- 2020 to 2022 is the documentation-shift test; table 16 showed no care unit with complete features in it (the one unit at 98% has 59 stays), so no clean subgroup of that era exists, and table 17 showed the gap opens on a date inside the window rather than by unit or era.
- The gap itself: about 60% of the 2020 to 2022 ICU stays in MIMIC-IV v3.1 have no inputevents or procedureevents rows while their chartevents are complete, and the share falls to zero for admissions two or more years after a 2020 to 2022 anchor year. It is not in the v3.0 or v3.1 release notes; it belongs in the deck as a data finding and, as a courtesy to other users, in a mimic-code discussion with tables 14, 16 and 17 as the evidence.
