# Sanity run: hypotheses, evidence, verdicts

Two designs are documented here in the order they happened. Sections A to J and the first cohort run belong to the hospital-wide design; they are the evidence that led to the ICU design (the "Why the ICU" section) and every verdict in them still holds for the tables the ICU design reads. The ICU sections (K to N2 and the rebuilt 01) are listed last with their hypotheses; their "what the data showed" column is filled in from the next real run.

Every id, unit, code family, string and vocabulary the four SQL files depend on was stated as a hypothesis, tested with a read-only query in `sql/00_sanity_itemids.sql` against MIMIC-IV v3.1 on BigQuery (9 October 2026), and kept, refined or refuted on the numbers. All figures are aggregates over the full database; none is a patient-level value. The tables themselves are in `docs/sanity_results/`, one CSV per section as BigQuery returned them. A peer can re-run any section in the BigQuery console (each scans whole tables, 60 to 80 GiB for the file; see the cost section of `docs/design.md`), and its tables should reproduce the numbers here.

## The cross-check that ties it together

Two tables built from different columns should agree. Section C counts 1,485,288 administrations with child rows (a product was given). Section D's "given" events sum to 1,459,046 (Administered, Delayed Administered, Administered in Other Location, Partial Administered, the three Confirmed variants); add about 18,000 NULL-event rows that carry a dose (G) and about 3,000 truncated " in Other Location" rows, and the total is about 1,480,000. Agreement to 0.4% is the strongest single piece of evidence that the five-way classification of event_txt carves the data correctly.

## What the sanity run changed, in one paragraph

The item ids were all right. The model of the medication table was wrong three times: the dose is on the child rows, drips are recorded as Started/Stopped intervals rather than events, and "Not Given per Sliding Scale" means nothing was due rather than a dose was held. A fourth hypothesis, that NULL event rows were never acted on, was refuted by its own test. Each refutation added or corrected a feature before any model saw a row, and each would have been invisible afterwards: a dose feature that is silently all zeros still produces an AUROC.

## Stated limits

- Fingerstick values charted as 0 (146 readings) are excluded by the floor; most are probably meter "LO" readings, so some true severe events are lost. 01 reports how many fall in an at-risk label window.
- "Confirmed" (58,450 rows) was read as self-administered insulin on the strength of D2. The first ICU run refuted it (`docs/cohort_findings.md`, table 13): the rows are infusion checks, and the second design reads them as drip evidence. The remaining limit is the reverse one: an eMAR interval closed by the 48 h cap rather than a Stopped overstates the drip, and table 15 measures how often that happens.
- The 48 h infusion cap is read off the Stopped-closed distribution; it closed 22% of intervals in the first run, and 01 table 8 reports how many at-risk rows a 72 h cap would add.
- Ward rows are labelled from venous lab glucose only; the ward event rate is an undercount of true hypoglycemia and the model's ward predictions are predictions of lab-detected events. ICU rows, with fingerstick monitoring, are the better-measured subgroup and are reported on their own.
- Dose caps remove a handful of impossible insulin doses; the tabular features sum the capped value. dose_given_raw is kept so the choice can be audited.

## Why the ICU (the pivot, 10 October 2026)

The hospital-wide label was lab-detected hypoglycemia on the wards. Three facts settled it: (1) section J will show every glucose-like item in both dictionaries, and the fingerstick item (225664) lives in `icu.chartevents` only; the hosp dictionary has the chemistry and blood-gas glucose; (2) the mimic-code question asking where ward fingersticks are (discussion #1174) has been unanswered since 2021; (3) the first 01 run found 0.66% of at-risk days with a result below 54. In the ICU, glucose is charted every 1 to 4 h, insulin drips sit in `inputevents` with every rate change and explicit end times, and feeds and dextrose are charted, so both CMS harm events (< 54 and > 300) are measured. The study was re-scoped to ICU days on insulin; the landmark design, the CMS816 rules, the leakage rules, the serialization, the model arms and the evaluation protocol are unchanged.

## Risk factors added after the fact check (10 October 2026)

The fact check of the feature table against the ICU hypoglycemia literature found three named risk factors with no column: renal replacement therapy (Vriesendorp 2006: CVVH with bicarbonate-based replacement fluid, OR 14), infection or sepsis (Vriesendorp 2006 OR 2.2; Krinsley and Grover 2007; Waeschle 2008, where severe hypoglycemia rose from 2.1% to 11.5% with sepsis severity), and octreotide given alongside insulin (Vriesendorp 2006 OR 6.0, wide interval). MIMIC-IV carries all three, so they were added rather than listed as limits:

- Dialysis: `procedureevents` intervals plus `chartevents` CRRT settings and hemodialysis output, merged per stay (00 O2: CRRT charting covers 2,927 stays against 2,274 with an interval and all but 3 of those; the two hemodialysis sources overlap on only 53% of their union; and "Dialysis Catheter" matches the label regex without being dialysis). Features `rrt_at_tk`, `crrt_at_tk`, `rrt_24h`, `ihd_24h`, `rrt_hours_24h`. The replacement-fluid composition in Vriesendorp's finding is not charted in MIMIC-IV, so the feature is dialysis itself, not its fluid.
- Infection: the `inputevents` "Antibiotics" category (617k one-minute administrations, antivirals and antifungals included) and `hosp.microbiologyevents` specimens without the MRSA admission screen and the serology panels, with the Sepsis-3 idea of suspicion (antibiotics plus a culture) folded into one 24 h look-back so the flag cannot reach past the landmark. No sepsis label and no severity score is computed: SOFA would be built from the labs and vitals the model already sees, and a diagnosis code for sepsis is a discharge code (leakage). `suspected_infection_24h` is a proxy for the start of a work-up, not for the presence of sepsis, and the deck says so.
- Octreotide: eMAR `drug_class` 'octreotide' and an `inputevents` label match, union of sources, as for insulin (00 L).

The spec's limits list and the deck's mechanism slide name what remains out of reach: feed energy (mL, not kcal), the insulin-pump dose, and clinician review of the feature table.
